Where Julia AGREES</h2><p>Every decidable-by-finite-enumeration Lean theorem is reproduced exactly, in every exact carrier:</p><ul> <li>All 29 C6_Twist battery lines: the four-row table, fermion σ-trivialisation with the explicit gauge witness, twistedBoson's four properties, sliceI's three, the fake-cocycle negative control, reality lemmas, |B³| = 16/32, |ker d²| = 16/8, B³ ⊆ Z³ both readings.</li> <li>All 15 C6_Gap lines: seal at μ₄, the pinch at (8,3) with all four witnesses, no-pinch at (8,7), <code>sqrtsOfOne</code>, total pinch at μ₃/μ₅, the odd asymmetry.</li> <li>All 6 hexagon lines: boson braids real, fermion braids imaginary, σ fixes / swaps the braidings, the self-inverse edict and its failure at ±i.</li> </ul><p>This is expected and is itself informative: for <strong>finite, decidable</strong> claims, Lean's <code>by decide</code> and Julia's <code>all(...)</code> compute <em>the same fold over the same finite set</em>. On this territory the two languages are equally strong, and Julia is strictly cheaper (0.15 s vs 46 s — the Lean kernel evaluates unary-ish terms through a proof checker; Julia JIT-compiles to machine code).</p><h2>3. Where Julia DISAGREES</h2><p>Three genuine disagreements surfaced while translating, each with a runnable demonstration:</p><p><strong>(a) Equality is plural, and floats break theorems.</strong> Carrier 8 models the same four points as <code>exp(ikπ/2)</code> in <code>ComplexF64</code>. With exact <code>==</code>, 13 of 29 theorems <strong>fail</strong> — e.g. <code>i*i = -1 + 1.2246e-16im ≠ -1</code>, so the fermion is no longer even a cocycle and <code>B³</code> counts drift. Switch the equality to <code>isapprox</code> and all 29 return. Lean's single definitional/propositional equality makes this entire failure mode inexpressible — which is precisely why it proves things and Julia only computes them.</p><p><strong>(b) Default integers are a different ring.</strong> <code>typemax(Int64) + 1</code> silently equals <code>typemin(Int64)</code>. A "∀-checked" Julia claim over <code>Int</code> is a claim about Z/2⁶⁴, not Z. Lean's <code>Nat</code>/<code>Int</code> are arbitrary-precision <em>inside the logic</em>; Julia has <code>BigInt</code> but you must reach for it, and nothing warns you when you don't.</p><p><strong>(c) Evidence vs proof — the disagreement about what "verify" means.</strong> Julia checking 65536 cases produces 65536 facts. Lean's <code>twist_invisible_on_fixed</code> is one term quantifying over <strong>every type M, every mul, every inv, every σ, every F</strong> — uncountably many instances, closed by <code>congrArg</code>, checked in microseconds. Julia cannot state that sentence, only sample it (Part G samples it at Z/6, which Lean never mentioned — instantly, but only as one more instance). Symmetrically: Julia has no <code>#print axioms</code>, no kernel, no distinction between "evaluated true" and "proved". Its answer to "does this depend on any axiom?" is: <em>the question does not typecheck here.</em></p><h2>4. Where Julia EXTENDS</h2><p><strong>(a) The sweep Lean's kernel could not decide.</strong> <code>NOT-PROVED.md</code> §1 records that the full 4⁸ = 65536-symbol sweep was <code>#eval</code>-only — two kernel runs died, and the naive recursion SIGABRTs the kernel at depth 16384. Julia decides it in <strong>0.13 seconds</strong>: |Z³| = 32 untwisted, 64 twisted, 8 shared, and pentagon ↔ untwisted-cocycle holds as a set identity over all 65536 symbols. Every <code>#eval</code>-claimed number is confirmed, and the quotient the Lean artifact never states falls out: |H³| = 32/16 = <strong>2</strong> untwisted and 64/32 = <strong>2</strong> twisted — the group-count form of "the classes merge but the count survives." (Status honesty: this is computation, not kernel proof. It upgrades the artifact's <code>#eval</code> claims to independently-reimplemented, cross-carrier computation — not to theorems.)</p><p><strong>(b) Representation plurality as a first-class value.</strong> In Lean, relating two representations costs four theorems (<code>toGZ_mul</code>, <code>toGZ_conj</code>, <code>toGZ_inj</code>, <code>is_unit</code> — the "bridge" section of the build stack). In Julia a representation is a <em>value</em> (a named tuple of <code>mul/inv/σ/eq/els</code>), and <code>iso_report</code> machine-checks the bridge — hom, σ-equivariance, inverses, unit, injectivity — for all nine carriers in a loop. Adding a tenth representation costs five lines and zero proofs.</p><p><strong>(c) Meaning-transport.</strong> Each carrier makes a different Lean theorem <em>visible</em> in its own vocabulary:</p><ul> <li>log form: <code>conj_eq_inv</code> stops being a theorem and becomes <strong>definitional</strong> — both maps are literally <code>x -&gt; mod(-x,4)</code>;</li> <li>matrices: σ = <strong>transpose</strong>, so "conjugation inverts the units" becomes "for rotation matrices, the transpose is the inverse" — an orthogonality fact;</li> <li>permutations: σ <em>has</em> to be permutation inversion for the bridge to close — the carrier itself re-derives <code>conj_eq_inv</code>;</li> <li>rational angles: the "unit circle" becomes exact instead of floating. Lean states each fact once, in one vocabulary, forever. Julia lets the same fact wear seven costumes, and <em>checking that the costumes fit</em> (the iso report) is itself a computation.</li> </ul><p><strong>(d) Free generality via parametric types.</strong> The hexagon plumbing written once runs unmodified over <code>Complex{Int}</code>, <code>Complex{BigInt}</code>, <code>Complex{Rational{BigInt}}</code>. Lean's hand-rolled <code>GZ</code> fixed <code>Int</code> coefficients; generalising it would mean re-engineering the structure and re-proving. This is duck-typed genericity: broader reach, zero guarantees.</p><h2>5. What I noticed while translating</h2><ol> <li><strong>Lean's rigidity is load-bearing.</strong> Translating <code>d3</code> I had to get the exact face order and the leading-face σ placement right. Lean would have <em>caught</em> a transposition only if some theorem then failed — but the theorems are the spec, and there are 66 of them; my Julia had to match all 66 across 9 carriers before I trusted the transcription. The theorem battery, not the type system, is what made the translation checkable. An artifact-first lesson: <strong>ship the test battery with the definitions.</strong> </li> <li><strong><code>decide</code> and <code>all(...)</code> are the same algorithm with different receipts.</strong> Both fold a predicate over a finite enumeration. Lean's receipt is a kernel certificate depending on zero axioms; Julia's receipt is an exit code. The <em>computational</em> content is identical; the <em>epistemic</em> content is not. </li> <li><strong>Where Lean was slow, the reason was foundational, not incidental.</strong> 46 s for C6_Twist vs 0.15 s in Julia is the price of running arithmetic through a proof checker. And the kernel's SIGABRT at the 65536 sweep (documented in NOT-PROVED.md, with the logarithmic-recursion workaround) shows the boundary is engineering-shaped: Lean <em>can</em> decide big finite sweeps, but you must shape recursion for the kernel's stack. Julia never asks you to; it also never certifies you. </li> <li><strong>Julia's plurality cuts both ways.</strong> Nine carriers means nine chances to notice a theorem from a new angle (transpose-as-inverse was invisible in Lean's vocabulary) — and nine chances to import a footgun (floats, Int64 wrap, <code>==</code> vs <code>===</code>). Lean's "very specific" single form is exactly what makes its 66 statements unambiguous. </li> </ol><h2>6. The summary sentence</h2><blockquote> <p><strong>Julia can say the same thing many ways; Lean can say it only one way, but when Lean says it, it is true.</strong> Julia's plurality of carriers is real mathematical optionality — it re-derived every Lean theorem seven exact ways, exposed each theorem in new vocabularies, and decided in 0.13 s a sweep the Lean kernel could not survive. But every one of those results is <em>evidence with an exit code</em>, quantified only over the instances actually run, on carriers where equality itself is negotiable. Lean's one form is <em>proof with a certificate</em>, quantified over all instances at once, with the axiom ledger printed. The two languages agree wherever a claim is finite and exact; they part company exactly at infinity (only Lean can go there) and at the machine (only Julia admits it lives on one).</p> </blockquote><hr><p><em>Reproduce: <code>julia julia/run_all.jl</code> — exit 0 iff every claim above that is a computation checks. The Lean side: <code>lean lean/C6_Twist.lean &amp;&amp; lean lean/C6_Gap.lean</code>.</em></p>
